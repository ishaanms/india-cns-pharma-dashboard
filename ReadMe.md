India produces the world's antidepressants. Most MNC originators have quietly stopped selling them here.

I spent this weekend building a market intelligence dashboard on India's CNS pharma market — using verified retail prices from Netmeds and public data from WHO, AIOCD, and PLFS.

A few things that surprised me:

→ Generic CNS drugs in India cost between ₹96–₹392/month. Less than a streaming subscription for a chronic condition. Price is not the access barrier.

→ The treatment gap for depression is 85% — 56 million Indians untreated. The bottleneck is psychiatrist density (0.3 per 100k vs WHO norm of 1) and stigma. Not cost.

→ Domestic generics (Sun, Torrent, Alkem) control ~84% of the CNS market. Most MNC originators — Zyprexa, Zoloft — aren't meaningfully present on Indian retail platforms at all.

→ The one exception: Lundbeck's Cipralex, at ₹268/month vs ₹156 for the generic equivalent. A 1.7× gap — not the 15–20× you'd see in the US. India has compressed MNC pricing to near-parity.

The real strategic question isn't pricing. It's: as Tier 2-3 cities develop specialist infrastructure, do MNCs re-enter the mass market — or stay in their narrow, high-margin niche?
